Individual Meeting Guideline

An individual meeting is not a weekly checkpoint. It is a focused research discussion where we clarify the problem, sharpen the method, examine the evidence, and decide the next step together.

Please come with materials, thoughts, and questions, so the meeting can move your research forward rather than only record what has happened.

01 / Objective

What the meeting is for

The meeting should be used for important research decisions: problem formulation, methodology, experiment design, result analysis, paper writing, and the plan for the next stage.

It should not become only this

A simple progress report where we only check current model performance, unfinished experiments, or whether the numbers look better than last week.

It should become this

A deep discussion based on concrete materials, where we identify the bottleneck, improve the design, clarify the writing, and make the next action more precise.

02 / Mindset

Bring a discussion, not only a result

A good meeting begins before the meeting. Please think carefully about what you want to discuss and what feedback you need.

1

State the key question

What is the most important research question or technical uncertainty you want to resolve in this meeting?

2

Explain your reasoning

Why do you believe the current design is reasonable? What challenge does it address?

3

Use evidence

Which preliminary experiments, observations, examples, or failed attempts support your current decision?

03 / Preparation

Prepare materials that can be discussed

Slides are useful for temporary ideas and fast discussion. The Overleaf draft is more important when the idea needs to be formalized and remembered.

✓

Slides or updated draft

Bring visible materials. Do not rely only on oral explanation.

✓

Clear technical details

Use equations, definitions, figures, or algorithmic descriptions whenever possible.

✓

Motivation for each design

Explain why each component is needed and why simpler solutions are insufficient.

✓

Concrete next-week plan

Prepare specific deliverables instead of vague statements such as “continue experiments.”

04 / Methodology

For methodology discussions

At the method-development stage, please make the logic visible: challenge, design, formalization, and evidence should be connected.

Step 1

Technical details

Describe the method clearly enough that another student could re-implement the core idea.

Step 2

Formalization

Write down the key definitions, equations, objectives, constraints, or algorithmic process.

Step 3

Motivation

Explain what challenge each design addresses and what benefit it brings.

Step 4

Preliminary evidence

Use observations, pilot experiments, examples, or ablations to make the idea convincing.

05 / Experiments

For experiment discussions

Experiments are not only numbers. Each experiment should answer a research question and support a claim in the paper.

1

Settings

Include datasets, baselines, metrics, implementation details, and important hyperparameters.

2

Motivation

Explain why this experiment is necessary and which research question it is designed to answer.

3

Observation

Summarize what we can learn from the result, why it happens, and whether it supports the paper.

06 / Draft Culture

Treat the draft as part of research

Updating the draft is not a final-stage polishing task. It is how we force ourselves to clarify the research logic.

Writing is not only the final step of a paper. Writing is part of the research process.

Problem formulation Writing it down forces you to define exactly what problem you are solving.
Methodology Formalizing the method exposes unclear assumptions and missing details.
Experiments Writing RQs and settings makes baselines, metrics, fairness, and analysis more precise.

Next-week plan

Every meeting should end with a concrete plan. The plan should specify the task, the reason, and the expected deliverable.

What will be done?

Example: Complete the ablation study for the representation alignment module on two datasets.

Why is it important?

Example: This experiment verifies whether the module is necessary for the main performance gain.

What should be delivered?

Example: A table, a short observation paragraph, and the corresponding update in the Overleaf draft.

Please take the individual meeting seriously.

My expectation is not that every meeting must already have perfect results. My expectation is that every meeting should help us move the research forward, by clarifying the problem, improving the method, strengthening the experiments, or sharpening the writing.
